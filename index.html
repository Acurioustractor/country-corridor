Our Vision

We envision a future where First Nations communities along the Alice Springs-Tennant Creek corridor are thriving on their own terms - with strong cultural connections, sustainable housing, meaningful employment opportunities, and self-determined services that truly meet community needs.

This initiative brings together the storytelling and human-centered design expertise of A Curious Tractor, the funding and philanthropic experience of The Snow Foundation, and the innovative community-led approaches of organizations like Wilya Janta to support existing local leaders and initiatives.

Our Approach

1. Indigenous Leadership First

Every initiative begins with community invitation and is led by local First Nations people with deep cultural connections and community knowledge.

2. Connected Communities

We recognize the cultural, familial, and historical connections between communities along the corridor, fostering collaboration and knowledge sharing.

3. Flexible, Long-Term Support

We provide resources that are responsive to community-identified priorities with timeframes that allow for deep, sustainable change.

4. Amplifying Local Solutions

Rather than imposing external models, we identify and strengthen existing community-led initiatives that are already making a difference.

Core Components

Community Mapping & Asset Identification

Working with Elders and community leaders to map cultural knowledge, existing initiatives, local skills, and community assets across the corridor.

Regional Amplification Network

Creating structures that connect individual community efforts for greater impact, including a representative Corridor Council and regular knowledge exchange gatherings.

Flexible Funding Mechanisms

Designing funding approaches that genuinely support community ownership, including community-controlled funds, quick response grants, and long-term commitments.

Integrated Support Network

Providing complementary support systems that communities can access as needed, including technical assistance, storytelling infrastructure, and administrative support.

Success Stories

Wilya Janta: Standing Strong Through Housing

In Tennant Creek, Warumungu Elders and community leaders have revolutionized housing design through the Wilya Janta initiative. By combining cultural knowledge with sustainable building practices, they've created housing that truly meets community needs - with outdoor kitchens, shaded sleeping areas, and designs that maximize airflow to suit traditional living practices.

The Goods Project: Transforming Waste into Wellbeing

The Goods Project addresses twin challenges of plastic waste and lack of essential items by manufacturing high-quality, durable bedding from recycled materials. Community feedback shows these beds significantly improve sleep quality, safety, and overall comfort while creating local employment opportunities.

Oonchiumpa: Two Cultures, One World

This 100% Aboriginal-owned and operated organization has transformed outcomes for at-risk youth through their "Two Cultures, One World, Working Together" approach. By connecting young people with cultural mentors and practical support, they've seen remarkable reductions in justice system involvement and increased educational engagement.
